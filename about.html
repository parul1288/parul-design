About

Hi, I'm Parul —
designer who builds,
builder who designs.

I've spent eight years moving between design and engineering — close enough to both to know what gets lost between them.

I spent eight years at the intersection of design and engineering — starting as a frontend developer who cared about how things felt to use, then moving into roles where I could shape what we built and why. In product roles at startups, I owned work end to end: from early design conversations through to implementation and validation.

I stepped away from full-time work to focus on family — and used that time intentionally. I started The Chaska Life, an independent practice focused on experience design for real-world gatherings. It reminded me that the principles behind good UX aren't screen-specific. I came back with sharper instincts and a clearer sense of what I want to build.

What I bring

The rare bit:
fluent in both languages.

Most of my career has been about owning problems end to end — from early research through to what actually shipped. I've sat in the design conversation, written the implementation, watched users struggle with what we built, and gone back to fix it. That full loop changes how you design. You stop protecting the mockup and start caring about the outcome.

I work best in environments where design and engineering think together rather than hand off to each other. Not because I can do both, but because I've seen what happens when they don't.

Beyond the screen

Design lives in the
details you notice.

I think a lot about how people move through real spaces — how a table is set, how a gathering unfolds, how small decisions about light and order shape how people feel. Most of my sharpest UX instincts came from watching people navigate physical moments, not digital ones.

The Chaska Life

An independent practice in experience design for real-world gatherings.

Indian-inspired tablescapes, curated dinner experiences, and the considered placement of every detail. The same thinking that goes into a good user flow goes into a good evening — clarity, intention, and knowing when to get out of the way.
